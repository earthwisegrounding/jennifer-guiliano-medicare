Medicare Advantage

Also called Part C

A private plan that bundles hospital, medical, and usually drug coverage. Most use a provider network and have a yearly limit on what you pay out of pocket.

About Medicare Advantage

Medicare Supplement

Also called Medigap

Works alongside Original Medicare to help pay costs it doesn’t cover, like deductibles and coinsurance. Drug coverage is bought separately.

About Medicare Supplement

Prescription drug plans

Also called Part D

Coverage for your medications from a private plan. Often paired with Original Medicare and a Supplement. Plans differ in which drugs they cover and what they cost.

About drug plans

Quick answers

Common Questions

Does it cost anything to work with you?

No. There is no charge for my help. Like most Medicare agents, I'm paid by the insurance company if you enroll in a plan. A plan's premium is the same whether you enroll through me or on your own.

Are you part of Medicare or the government?

No. I'm a licensed, independent insurance agent. I'm not connected with Medicare, CMS, or Social Security. For official information you can always visit Medicare.gov or call 1-800-MEDICARE.

When should I start planning if I'm turning 65?

A good time to start is about 3 to 6 months before your 65th birthday. Your first chance to sign up — your Initial Enrollment Period — begins 3 months before the month you turn 65.

Can I keep my doctors?

It depends on the type of coverage. Original Medicare (with or without a Supplement) lets you see any provider nationwide who accepts Medicare. Medicare Advantage plans usually use networks. I can check your doctors before you choose.
